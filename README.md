# Paradise Island — a living biome

A procedural 3D island you can observe from any angle, built with Three.js.

**Run it:** double-click `Open Paradise Island.bat`. It starts the local server, which the notebook needs to save notes, and opens http://localhost:5173. Opening `index.html` directly still shows the island, but the notebook is read-only.
Or serve it: `node serve.mjs` then visit http://localhost:5173

**Rebuild after editing `src/`:** `npm run build` (minified) or `npm run dev` (watch).

## What's on the island
- **Mount Ignis** – active volcano: a glowing lava lake in the crater (the flanks are cold, dark basalt; the climb to the rim is bare rock, with no ropes or fixed gear), an eruption every 24 seconds (the first about 12 seconds in), where fireballs climb and burst into glowing pieces like fireworks (some crackle into a second burst) before they can land, with an ash plume and camera shake. Trigger one any time with the button.
- **Veil Falls** – waterfall from a plateau lake into a plunge pool, with a hidden grotto behind the curtain. Plus the **Tide Cascade** pouring down the west slope into the sea.
- **Pearl Cove** – turquoise lagoon with coral, seagrass, a campfire, and the bones of a four-hundred-year-old treasure galleon (ribs bared, gun ports gaping, masts snapped, no sails).
- **South Pacific touches** – one traditional outrigger canoe (va'a, with ama float, lashed booms and a carved bow) drawn up at the waterline, a single thatched lean-to of poles and woven pandanus, a shallow coral garden (tables, branching, brain, anemones, sea stars, urchins) in clear turquoise water by the beach, two small motu islets with palms offshore, a belt of red/pink/yellow/orange hibiscus and white/cream/pink/yellow frangipani along the sand, and tropical birds: rainbow lorikeets in the palms, white terns over the lagoon (some perched on the dock and canoe), and frigatebirds overhead. Press **0** for the "South Seas" view.
- **Wildlife** – capuchin monkeys (they climb palms and knock down coconuts, and burst out of the undergrowth to snatch ones that fall or roll), wild boar, island rats, barn owls, Pacific boas, flamingos, crabs, scarlet macaws, seagulls, white terns, sea eagles, bats at dusk, butterflies, fireflies, dolphins, sea turtles, reef sharks, manta rays, a humpback whale that travels submerged and breaches often, leaping clear of the water, jellyfish, and 11 schools of fish.
- **The Castaway** — a blond, sun-tanned man on a daily rhythm: breakfast on a coconut at his campfire, long hikes all over the island (temple, hot spring, falls, far beaches…), swimming laps in the lagoon, then an afternoon fishing wherever there's water — and from about 1:30pm he drags out his outrigger canoe, paddles it across the lagoon, fishes from the cockpit, and paddles home (the canoe really moves; its paddles come off the hull and go into his hands) — rod from the dock, the beaches, the waterfall pool and the river banks, from the canoe, or wading the shallows with a spear. He keeps what he catches, grills it on a spit at his campfire at dusk, eats it, and sleeps in his palm-frond lean-to. Find him in the Field guide under *People*; his description says what he's up to.
  - **His mind** (`src/castaway_mind.js`, run by `src/human.js`) plays survival on its hardest setting, and plays it well. He has three live meters, shown under the title on the observation screen: **food, energy and happiness**. Water is not a need (the island has plenty), but he is seen drinking regularly, by habit, every few hours: kneeling at the river or waterfall pool (not the sea), or from a coconut he has run down and won from the monkeys, alternating the two. Hunger drains at about survival-mode rates, so he keeps ahead of it: he fishes, and at dusk creeps up on island rats and spears them, and **everything he catches is cooked at the fire before he eats it** (fish and rats on the spit). He sleeps in his lean-to and eats before bed. A critical need beats the rain, and he never drops what he is doing for a lesser need. His goal is happiness: once the basics are covered he goes after what lifts his spirits (somewhere new, wildlife, a swim, the canoe, a chase after a monkey). When he is happy he dances more, and whistles and sings as he goes about his day (synthesised, positional audio). **He runs** (a real run cycle) from boars, after monkeys for fun, and on long legs to a new place. Boars react to him as well as to the camera: the silver tusker and some wild boar bluff-charge, and he sprints to safety, then keeps clear of that patch for a while. Follow him from the heading of the meters panel.
  - **His model** is built in Blender (`blender/`, scripted and re-runnable; `castaway.blend` is the saved scene): a lean, weathered, grey-bearded man with shaggy sun-bleached hair, leathery skin (a pore/wrinkle shader), a torn linen loincloth with a second ragged panel, a rope belt and knot, and a driftwood staff lashed with cord and hung with a bone fish-hook, skinned to the game's own joint hierarchy so all his animations drive it. To change him, edit `blender/cast_*.py`, run `run_all.py` then `cast_export.py` in Blender (it writes `assets/castaway*.glb`), and `node build.mjs`. If the model can't load, the old procedural man is used.
- **The castaway's inner life** — beyond food, rest and a drink, he has eight drives (adventure, curiosity, collecting, creating, creativity, building, play, hunting) that turn into goals with real steps: he notices unusual stones by a waterfall and later goes to look, picks up shells, stones, driftwood and feathers (they leave the world and join his collection), arranges them into spirals, cairns, arches and wind chimes that stay, works out new designs, builds a bench and a ring of stones by his fire, goes on long walks, and plays on the beach. He remembers places, finds and frights, learns what he enjoys, and saves all of it. The new Blender castaway (`assets/castaway/`, 142 animation clips, face controls) acts it out. See `docs/CASTAWAY_LIFE.md` and `docs/CASTAWAY_ANIMATION_COVERAGE.md`.
- **Boars and feral pigs** — modelled in Blender (`blender/boar_*.py`, `boar.blend`; run `boar_all.py` in Blender to rebuild, colour and export `assets/boar.glb`, then `npm run build`): a wedge-shaped body with a bristle mane, cloven hooves, tusks, ears, tail and a hinged jaw. Five kinds and sizes: grizzled wild boar (sows and boars), yearlings, the huge solitary silver tusker, ginger boars and spotted feral pigs, plus striped piglets. Each has its own temper: wild boar keep their distance and bolt, the tusker stands his ground, champs his jaws and bluff-charges, and the ginger and spotted pigs wander over to look at you. Their day has a rhythm: they root and dig at dawn and dusk, wallow in the mud at the river in the midday heat, scratch on palm trunks, drink, and sleep in a heap at night with the piglets tucked in beside the sow. In survival mode any adult boar can charge you after dark. Code in `src/boar.js` (behaviour and kinds) and `src/boar_model.js` (the model).
- **The Crystal Cave** — the old grotto is now a hidden cave system behind Veil Falls (`blender/cave_build.py` builds the rock, the crystal and gem placements and the walkable floor; run it with `blender -b --python blender/cave_build.py`, then `npm run build`). Step through the doorway in the cliff behind the falls: a rugged Crystal Hall, a winding passage and the domed Heart chamber round a glowing pool. About 900 bioluminescent crystals in eight colours, 170 embedded gemstones, glowing moss, mineral veins that pulse, stalactites and stalagmites, drips falling into the pool, and drifting motes. The cave is hollow under the cliff: its floor is a baked grid (`caveFloorAt` in `world.js`), the doorway is cut out of the terrain mesh, and the survival camera does not pull in under the rock.
- **Fruit bats** — a colony of 42 flying foxes (`blender/bat_build.py`, `fruitbat.blend`; `src/bat_model.js`, `src/bats.js`). The Blender model has a fox face, big ears, a golden mantle and four-fingered wings, plus a second, roosting pose (wings wrapped like a cloak) stored in a `_fold` attribute; the wing beat is done in the vertex shader (deep downstroke, wrist flexed on the upstroke, tips trailing), so the whole colony is one draw call. They hang upside down in clusters on the hall ceiling and groom, stretch a wing, fan themselves in the midday heat, shuffle and squabble with neighbours. If you walk into the hall the startle spreads from the nearest bats outward like a wave: each drops, unfurls and flaps, they mill round the hall, then stream out through the cave mouth and over the falls, range over the island, and come home to their own roost spots. They also leave together at dusk to feed through the night in the palms, and return at dawn.
- **Crabs** — modelled in Blender (`blender/crab_*.py`, `crab.blend`): a crab with a toothed, ridged carapace, eye stalks, eight jointed-looking legs and two big claws with moving pincers that nip open and shut. They come in four kinds and sizes: red beach crabs (medium), pale ghost crabs (small, fast), hermit crabs in spiral shells (tiny, slow, tuck in when you near) and big purple coconut crabs under the palms. Most other animals now vary in size too (monkeys, boars, flamingos, birds).
- **Island rats, barn owls and Pacific boas** — three animals modelled in Blender (`blender/rat_build.py`, `owl_build.py`, `boa_build.py`, sharing `creature_lib.py`; they write `assets/island_rat.glb`, `barn_owl.glb` and `pacific_boa.glb`, then `npm run build`). The **island rat** (`src/rat.js`) lives in burrows at the foot of the palms, comes out at dusk, forages, sniffs, freezes at a shadow overhead and bolts for a burrow; it is a food source for the omnivores (boars snap them up, and the castaway spears and roasts them). The **barn owl** (`src/owl.js`) roosts in a tree by day and quarters low over the ground at night, hovers over a rat and drops on it, and screeches. The **Pacific boa** (`src/boa.js`) lies coiled by day, slithers out at dusk tasting the air, stalks a rat, strikes, constricts, swallows it whole (the body bulges) and digests. The shared registry is `src/ecology.js`. The Field Guide has entries and live facts for all three.
- **Coconut rivalry** — coconuts that fall or roll are fair game. Monkeys sprint out of the foliage to snatch them (`Monkey` in `src/fauna_land.js`), carry them off in both arms and eat them; the castaway races them for a coconut when he wants a drink (`window.__dbg.flora.coconuts.free/take` in `src/flora.js`). Nuts taken from under a palm grow back.
  - **White terns** are modelled in Blender too (`blender/tern_*.py`, `assets/tern.glb`): a slim white tern with long pointed wings, a forked tail, a straight black bill and short dark legs, and a juvenile plumage (grey-brown back with pale flecks) for about one bird in seven. They fly the lagoon as one flock of thirteen: pairs keep close together, the flock circles the coral, and now and then a bird hovers and plunges onto the water after a fish, with the birds near it following the dive. When the flock rests, three of them land on the dock posts, where pairs turn their heads to each other and preen. Frigatebirds and sea eagles over the lagoon are mobbed by the flock, and walking up to the flock flushes it. To change them, edit the `tern_*.py` scripts, run `tern_build.py` and `tern_color.py` in Blender, then `tern_export.py`, and `npm run build`. If the model can't load, the old procedural bird is used.
  - **Flamingos** are modelled in Blender too (`blender/flamingo_*.py`, `flamingo.blend`): a long-necked greater flamingo with a bent bill whose lower half hinges, backward-bending hocks and folded wings. Four plumages, all within what real birds show: rosy classic adults, pale adults, deep coral-red adults and grey-white juveniles with dark bills. They move as one flock of seven: they feed on the shallows and mud with the head upside down, sweeping the bill, and drift about while they feed. Now and then the flock walks to a nearby pool or runs across the water with its wings going, then lifts off at a shallow angle and flies (wings beating, neck out, legs trailing) over the jungle to a pool across the island; on landing it runs on to a stop and spreads out again. Walking up to them sends the whole flock into the air. Individually they also preen, flick their wings, and stand on one leg. To change them, edit the `flamingo_*.py` scripts, run `flamingo_build.py`, `flamingo_color.py`, then `flamingo_export.py` in Blender (it writes `assets/flamingo.glb`), and `npm run build`. If the model can't load, the old procedural flamingo is used.
- **Weather** — mostly sunny, with a wandering breeze that sweeps in gust fronts across the grass and palms, and every so often clouds roll in for rain showers or a thunderstorm (wind, lightning, thunder, rain on the sea, wet ground). Force a mood in the View tab: Auto / Sun / Rain / Storm. The castaway runs for his lean-to when it rains.
- **South Pacific plants** — coconut palms (full crowns, fruit clusters, dead fronds), pandanus screw pines with stilt roots and orange fruit, giant banyans with aerial roots (about a dozen: the temple, the castaway's camp, the lake, the spring and the fairy glade each get a shade tree), breadfruit, plumeria; beach naupaka, hibiscus in six colours, bougainvillea, heliconia, bird of paradise, ginger torch, taro, ti plants, morning glory, orchids and meadow wildflowers.
- **7 secrets** to discover by exploring (Secrets tab keeps track).
- Day/night cycle, living wind in the palms and grass, ocean with foam and caustics, procedural ambient sound.

## Camera (explorer style)
| Action | Control |
|---|---|
| **Free camera** (the default) | `W` `A` `S` `D` move relative to where you look; `R` / `F` rise and sink; `Q` / `E` turn; drag to look around. Nothing stops you: the camera goes wherever you take it |
| Zoom (lens) | mouse wheel, or `Z` / `X`: scroll in narrows the field of view, scroll out widens it; the camera itself doesn't move. The View tab's *Field of view* slider shows the same value |
| Follow an animal | click it, or pick it in the Field guide: the camera glides to a spot behind it and keeps it in view. Any movement key, or a drag, lets go and leaves you where you are (`Esc` also releases) |
| Go to a spot | double-click it; the camera glides there |
| Pan with the mouse | right-drag, middle-drag or `Shift`+drag |
| **First-person walk** | `V`, click to capture the mouse, `WASD` walk, `Shift` run, `Space` jump, `C` crouch (dive when swimming), `Q`/`E` turn, `1`–`9` teleport to a place, `V`/`Esc` to leave |
| Faster / slower | hold `Shift` / `Ctrl` |
| Places | `1`–`9` or the dock at the bottom of the screen (the camera glides there) |
| Hide UI / pause time / mute | `H` / `P` / `M` |

**Controller** (an Xbox 360 / One pad, or anything the browser maps as a standard gamepad; press any button once so the browser sees it): left stick moves, right stick looks (it eases off when the lens is zoomed in or **X** is held), **RT** rises and **LT** sinks, hold **A** for fast and **X** for precise, d-pad up/down zooms the lens, d-pad left/right steps through the places, **R3** resets the lens, **Start** hides the interface, **Back** toggles first-person walking (left stick walks, **A** jumps, **B** crouches, **X** takes hold on the climb, **RT** or **L3** runs).

**Locked on:** **Y** locks the camera on to whatever is nearest the middle of the view, and **LB** / **RB** step to the previous / next creature around it (clicking a creature does the same). A locked camera keeps its target dead centre: the right stick, the mouse (drag) and the keys swing the camera around it, the triggers / wheel-free left stick Y / W S dolly in and out, and the camera never dips below the ground. Nothing nudges it loose. To let go, hold **B** for about half a second (a bar fills on the card) or press **Esc** / the Release button.

Walking collides with trunks, boulders and pillars, climbs the temple steps, wades the shallows and swims the sea (dive underwater with `C`); lava and the crater are off limits. Crabs scuttle and monkeys back away as you approach, and every surface (sand, grass, rock, stone, wood, water) has its own footsteps.

## Survival mode
Press **▶ Play survival** on the observation screen. You are the castaway, washed up at the cove; the observation castaway steps aside until you leave. Build a camp, keep your needs up and make a raft to sail home.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `W` `A` `S` `D` / `Shift` / `Space` | move / run / jump (swim in deep water) |
| mouse drag, wheel, arrow keys | orbit and zoom the camera |
| `E` / `F` | the main and second action offered at the bottom of the screen (pick up, shake a palm, drink, cook, sleep, fish...) |
| `Tab` | bag and crafting (recipes unlock as you level up) |
| `1`-`9` | eat a food item from the bag strip |
| `Q` / `R` / `T` | drink from the canteen / turn a building while placing it / light or put out the torch |
| `Esc` | pause (resume, "I am stuck - back to camp", save and leave, start over) |

**Needs:** hunger, thirst and energy drain over game time (a day lasts about 10 real minutes). Empty bars hurt your health; a full set slowly heals it. Raw fish, crab and clams can upset your stomach (sickness drains you faster), so cook them; sea water is not drinkable, so find fresh water. Sleep in a shelter when it is dark or you are tired.

**Loop:** gather sticks, stones, fibre, clams and coconuts (look for sparkles) -> craft a stone axe, rope and a campfire -> fish, cook, dry fish on a rack -> chop driftwood into timber -> build a shelter, then a raft. Boars charge at night; keep a fire and a spear. A quest card guides the first steps, and each new day a supply crate washes up. Eight places (falls, temple, spring, fairy glade, lake, old camp, wreck, lava) give XP when found. The game saves automatically when you leave.

The code lives in `src/survival/` (`game.js` is the rules, `items.js` the items, recipes and quests, `nodes.js` the resources, `structures.js` the buildings, `actions.js` what E/F do, `hud.js` the interface). It borrows walking physics from `src/walk.js` and pauses the observation castaway while you play.

## Water
One shared surface model (`src/ripples.js`, `src/splash.js`) drives the sea, the streams, the lake, the plunge pool and the hot spring.

- **Ripples:** a pool of travelling ring waves that every splash, wake, raindrop and the waterfall feeds. The ocean and stream shaders read the same pool, so a splash looks the same on the sea and in a stream: bending light on the rings, a foam crest, and a foam patch where it landed. Rings only go in near the camera, and the shader cost stays flat however many are alive.
- **Splashes** (`fx.splash(x, y, z, size, {dir})`): sit on the real surface (tide, swell, stream or pond level), throw a crown of droplets and a central jet, lean the way the thing was travelling, and the droplets start tiny rings as they fall back in. Sizes run from a float (0.15) through a footstep (0.2), a dolphin (1 to 2), to a whale breach (3 to 5), with sound to match: a plip, a splash, or a deep thump and a wash.
- **Wakes** (`wake(...)`): dolphins, turtles, the shark's fin, the whale, swimmers, waders, the castaway, and a reeled-in fishing float each leave a V of rings. Dolphins and whales splash where they actually cross the surface; gulls splash down and lift off, paddle, and plunge for fish; terns splash on their dives; coconuts float on the swell and the tide, and drift rings.
- **Fishing:** the float rides the swell (at the stream's level in a stream), rings spread from it, a fish noses it just before it bites (quick small rings and a twitch: your cue), the bite is a strong splash with rings, and reeling in drags a wake.
- **Where waters meet:** each river mouth spreads its fresh water over the sea in a greenish plume with a streaky mixing front; the falls keep the plunge pool churning with rings; ponds and streams have wet, darker banks.
- **Shorelines:** shore foam keeps a constant width in metres whatever the slope of the bed (no sheet of white over a long flat at low tide); each swash slides up the sand and back, leaves lace foam as it retreats, and the sand behind it stays damp and glossy.
- **Rain** dimples every water surface near the camera with rings.

## Sound
Click **Enter paradise** to start. Everything is positional: waterfalls, surf, lava, campfire and temple torches, the grotto's crystal hum, hot-spring bubbles, and the animals' voices (monkeys, boar, macaws, gulls, flamingos, dolphins, the whale, crabs…) come from where they are. Night brings owls, frogs, crickets, bats and glimmer-ring chimes; the volcano warns, rumbles and booms; coconuts thud and splash.

**Music:** drop `.mp3 / .ogg / .wav / .m4a` files into the `music/` folder. They're picked up when you launch with `Open Paradise Island.bat` (or run `npm run build`). Volume sliders, track skip and toggles are in the View tab.

- **Sunrise & sunset** — the sky blazes through gold, orange, rose and violet bands with light shafts from the low sun, the belt of Venus and earth shadow opposite, clouds lit from beneath, and a swollen sun; sunrise is a softer peach and pink than sunset.


**Start screen:** `artwork/loading.jpg` (made from the PNG in `artwork/`) is the loading screen. The progress bar and *Press Start* plaque are painted in the art; the page lays live UI over them (a real progress fill, a status label, a clickable plaque that wakes up when the island is ready). Edit the positions in the `#boot` CSS in `index.html` if you replace the art.

**Volcano shake:** it erupts every 24 s, but the ground only shakes on about one eruption in five, or when you press *Erupt*.
- **Great frigatebirds** (from Codex, `blender/frigatebird/`, `assets/great_frigatebird_*.glb`, `src/frigatebird.js`): a colony of four roosts in the palm crowns (male, female, juvenile, and a second male) that perch, preen, display (the male inflates his red throat pouch), take off into the wind, soar on thermals, glide out to sea, stoop and snatch fish from the surface without touching the water, sometimes chase a gull, then return and land.
- **Boulders** come in nine shapes (`src/rocks.js`); the broad-crowned ones are lookouts: the castaway climbs up to sit, monkeys hop up to eat and look about, and gulls and terns rest on them. **Mirror Lake** has an irregular shoreline pulled back from the plateau edge.
